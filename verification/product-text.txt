S.01
About MetriX
A modular decision-support platform for non-ferrous and critical metals. MetriX AI connects mining operations, quality verification and secondary resource assessment through traceable data.
MetriX AI is at the prototype and proof-of-concept stage. We invite industry partners to validate one clear scenario before expanding the platform.

S.06
Validation Milestones
Product progress is assessed through reproducible simulations, expert review and scoped field pilots. These are validation priorities, not certifications or completed deployments.
Our validation priorities
01
Reproducible prototypes
Record input data, parameter versions and exceptions so the prototype results can be repeated and checked independently.
02
Expert-reviewed pilots
Use real scenario data, an agreed baseline and professional review to assess whether a module meets the pilot criteria.
03
Validated module delivery
Expand a validated module only when its scope, support requirements and repeatability are clear. Wider enterprise deployment is a proposed productisation direction.
04
Data & Assumptions
Record data sources, units, dates and model assumptions. Compare scenario results and require review when inputs fall outside the validated operating range.

S.03
Use Cases
Seven example use cases illustrate where a scoped pilot could begin. These are proposed applications, not customer deployments.
Map of our projects in Mexico
Route Planning
Mining Operators
MineFit AI compares feasible transport routes using distance, slope, congestion and energy assumptions. Operators review the expected time and cost before selecting a route. A pilot compares the recommendations with existing rules under the same tasks and fleet constraints.
Equipment Matching
Dispatch Teams
Match tasks to equipment using payload, position, availability and road access. MineFit AI first excludes infeasible choices, then compares workable combinations. Outdated equipment status and insufficient capacity are flagged for review.
Sampling Coverage
Testing Organisations
QualityGuard AI identifies differences in ore images and suggests sampling locations based on coverage, variability and budget. Visual clusters describe feature differences; they are not direct measurements of metal grade. Laboratory evidence and expert review remain essential.
Sample Traceability
Quality Teams
Link each sample to a batch, location, collection time and laboratory result. The planned workflow helps teams investigate unexpected results and identify missing coverage. Sample identifiers, handling and contamination controls must be validated for each field scenario.
Resource Screening
Recovery Businesses
CircularMine AI screens tailings and suitable by-products using dry quantity, measured grade, recovery assumptions and costs. It identifies candidates for additional tests. Original ore grades cannot substitute for the corresponding tailings measurements.
Recovery Comparison
Process Engineers
Compare suitable recovery pathways and observe how prices, recovery rates and costs affect their ranking. Estimated net operating value supports early screening; investment decisions require additional capital, financing and cash-flow analysis.
Rover Validation
Field Teams
The Smart Sampling Rover is a concept hardware extension. First validate mechanical sampling, storage and identifiers; then controlled mobility and remote operation; finally limited-area navigation and platform integration. The model illustrates the design, not certified performance.

S.04
Pilot Process
A pilot starts with a measurable scenario and agreed baseline. Prepare the data, run offline analysis, review the results with domain experts and expand only after the agreed indicators have been validated.
01
Define the scenario
Agree the operational question, boundaries, users and measurable acceptance criteria.
02
Prepare the data
Collect the relevant maps, equipment parameters, images, sample records or recovery assumptions.
03
Check the baseline
Record the current approach and distinguish measured data from simulated inputs.
04
Run offline analysis
Run the selected module against a fixed task and retain the input and parameter versions.
05
Review recommendations
Ask dispatch, sampling or process specialists to check recommendations against site conditions.
06
Compare scenarios
Compare the recommended options with the baseline using consistent units and constraints.
07
Test sensitivity
Change key prices, recovery rates or operating assumptions to understand result sensitivity.
08
Run a scoped pilot
Use reviewed recommendations in an agreed, limited scope and record actual outcomes.
09
Evaluate results
Check the agreed indicators, exceptions and applicability limits with the customer team.
10
Agree next steps
Decide whether the evidence supports continued use, additional validation or a revised scope.

Product principle
S.01
Evidence & Review
MetriX AI is at the prototype and proof-of-concept stage. Each module is designed to support professional judgment through explainable recommendations and traceable inputs.
Define the baseline, input quality and acceptance criteria before a pilot. Compare results under equivalent tasks and distinguish simulated values from measured outcomes.
Human review is a core product principle. A recommendation with insufficient evidence should request more data or pause.
S.02
Human Oversight
Important operational, sampling and recovery decisions stay with qualified people. The platform helps compare options and understand constraints.
Formal sampling plans and laboratory methods remain essential to confirming composition and quality.
Every result should state its applicable scope and the evidence still required.
S.03
Validation & Learning
Validation combines repeatable offline analysis with professional review and a limited, agreed field scope. Actual outcomes and exceptions inform the next iteration.
Track route cost and constraint violations, sampling coverage and test differences, or recovery calculation consistency according to the selected module.
Keep simulated inputs separate from measured data. Document sources, units, versions and assumptions alongside each result.
The proposed productisation approach connects four practical controls:
01
Data Provenance
Record source, collection time, units and applicable scope for each input.
02
Sample Traceability
Connect sample identifiers, batches, locations and laboratory results.
03
Model Review
Preserve model versions, assumptions and professional review decisions.
04
Data & Assumptions
Record data sources, units, dates and model assumptions. Compare scenario results and require review when inputs fall outside the validated operating range.

CircularMine AI: Compare Secondary Resource Value
Screen secondary resources
CircularMine AI compares potential recovery options for tailings and suitable industrial by-products. Use material-specific measurements and applicable recovery trial data.
Connect quantity, grade and recovery
Recoverable element quantity equals dry material quantity multiplied by element mass fraction and process recovery. Units must be consistent.
Compare revenue and operating costs
Estimated sales revenue uses saleable quantity, the relevant product price and any payable factor. Subtract processing, energy, transport, environmental treatment and other relevant operating costs.
Test the assumptions
Sensitivity analysis shows how price, recovery and cost changes affect the ranking of candidate pathways. Mark assumptions clearly and prioritise additional tests where evidence is weak.
Know the assessment boundary
Estimated net operating value supports screening. Investment feasibility also needs capital, schedule, tax, financing and cash-flow analysis. A high element content alone does not establish profitability.

MineFit AI: Match Equipment, Compare Routes
Match equipment to the task
MineFit AI considers payload, current position, equipment availability and road restrictions before comparing feasible assignments.
Compare routes consistently
Route analysis combines distance, slope, energy and congestion on a consistent scale. Estimated time and cost should show the input data and assumptions used.
Respond to scenario changes
A road closure, unavailable vehicle or revised task can trigger a new recommendation when the updated information is supplied. Offline analysis is not described as live dispatch.
Validate against a baseline
A pilot compares the current rules and module recommendations using identical tasks and fleet conditions. Route distance, task time, waiting time and constraint violations can inform acceptance.
Keep the operator in control
The first stage focuses on offline recommendations and simulation. Production system integration is a later scope; staff review recommendations before use.

QualityGuard AI: Better Sampling Starts with Coverage
Sampling starts with coverage
QualityGuard AI analyses visual feature differences across an ore pile and helps teams review where samples are collected. The goal is stronger evidence for quality decisions.
What the module uses
Area-labelled images, batch information, existing sampling points and matched laboratory results provide the input. Light, distance, dust and moisture must be controlled or documented.
What it recommends
Feature maps, sampling locations, suggested quantities and additional coverage help staff review a sampling plan within the agreed budget and access constraints.
What still needs testing
Visual clusters do not certify metal grade. Surface images cannot fully describe internal layers. Appropriate sampling methods, laboratory testing and professional review remain essential.
The field extension
The Smart Sampling Rover is a planned link between reviewed tasks, image collection and traceable samples. Hardware and operating conditions require separate validation.
